---
description: Brainstorm a brand film, reel or storytelling animation — concepts, arcs and openings, before any production
argument-hint: "[what it's for, e.g. 'reel for the new FMCG pitch']"
---

The user wants to **think**, not to execute. Everything else in brand-video assumes the brief already
exists; this is what happens before that. Do not start authoring a Video IR, and do not render anything.

## 1. Brand first, but lightly

Resolve and confirm the brand as usual (the **brand-kit** skill). Ideation still has to sit inside the
brand's voice, storyline arc and delivery formats. Keep it to the compact profile card — a brainstorm
does not need the full token cost of a production run.

If this is a **client** brand that is not registered yet, say so and offer `/brand-new`, but do not
block: you can brainstorm against a described brand and register it once a concept is chosen.

## 2. Shape the problem before generating anything

Read `skills/brand-video/references/brainstorm.md` now — it carries the question set, the format
catalogue, the divergence method and the concept shape. Follow it.

Use **AskUserQuestion** for what `$ARGUMENTS` has not already answered. The four that actually change
the output:

1. Who is watching, and where — a CEO in a pitch room, or a field supervisor on a phone in a store?
2. What one thing should change in their head?
3. The hard constraint — runtime, format, deadline, and what footage or data genuinely exists?
4. What must be true for them to believe it?

If the user cannot answer **#2**, stop and say so plainly. That is the real finding, and concepts
generated without it are decoration. Help them find the single decision first.

## 3. Generate, divergently

Three to four concepts, each from a **different angle** — not three variations of one idea. The angles
and the test are in the reference: *if two concepts would open on the same shot, they are one concept.*

Give each concept the fixed shape the reference specifies, so they can be compared honestly:

- the premise in one line
- the opening line, written as it would actually be spoken
- the arc beat by beat, mapped to motion templates (see `references/motion-templates.md`)
- the proof it rests on
- a runtime estimate at the brand's words-per-minute
- what it needs that does not exist yet
- the honest risk

## 4. Take a position

Lead with a recommendation and say why. Make the trade-off explicit — cheapest to make, most
persuasive, most reusable. Invite merging: the best film is often one concept's structure with
another's opening.

Never present four options and ask the user to choose blind. That is not a brainstorm, it is a menu.

## 5. Hand off

Once a concept is chosen:

1. Write the narration as **prose** and get it approved. The script is the film.
2. Then continue at step 4 of the **brand-video** workflow — author the Video IR, `--dry-run`, build,
   validate, watch.

Brainstorm output is not a brief until a concept is picked and the script is approved.

## Persisting what you learn

If the user rejects a direction for a reason that will recur — a format that never works for their
audience, a claim they are not allowed to make, an opening they always dislike — that is a durable
preference. Persist it with the learn protocol (`/brand-learn`) so the next brainstorm starts from it
instead of rediscovering it.
